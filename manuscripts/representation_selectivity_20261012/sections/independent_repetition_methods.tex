\subsection{Independent repetitions: design and execution}
\label{sec:independentrepetition}

The original PermWorld grid constitutes one exploratory experimental unit,
not 32 independent repetitions. An audit of the saved records recovered 64
genuine 20,000-update specialists and reproduced the reported counts from
the saved tables. In the joint-answer-controlled final-layer CKA analysis,
26/32 conditions have positive $B_t$ and 18/32 have positive $\Delta$;
Comparison B favors the designated task over the other-candidate mean in
29/32 conditions and ranks it uniquely first in 19/32. These are results
from the original unit. A repetition protocol specifies four additional
units. All five units are complete; their separate and combined results
are reported in Section~\ref{sec:independent-repetition-status}.

\paragraph{Random factors and paired controls.}
Each additional unit contains the same sixteen tasks, two encodings, and
two model configurations, requiring 64 specialists per unit and 256 new
specialists overall. Data sampling, the batch schedule, architecture-specific
initial parameters, and training random-number streams change between
units. Within a unit, all specialists use the same underlying permutations,
splits, and batch indices. Within each architecture, all tasks and both
encodings copy one saved numerical step-0 state; the parameter identity is
checked before fitting. Transformer and GRU initial parameters are separate.
The original training-stream dependence is retained: descents, inverse
descents, peaks, and valleys each have a task-specific stream within an
architecture, paired across encodings, while the other twelve tasks reuse
the descents stream of that architecture. Each new unit uses a new stream
base. Thus the repetitions jointly vary the sampled data, initial weights,
batch order, and training randomness, without separately estimating the
contribution of any one factor.

\paragraph{Sampling and execution.}
The original rejection sampler and fixed historical exclusion index are
retained. For each length $n=10,\ldots,30$, a unit has 4,096 training,
256 validation, and 512 test anchors. A single data-seeded generator produces
the three splits sequentially, accepting permutations whose eight-element
transformation orbits avoid the fixed historical index and previously
accepted orbits within that unit. No additional exclusion between units is
imposed; accidental cross-unit overlaps are permitted by the original
sampling mechanism. Evaluation has no separate random sampler: its anchors
are generated by the unit's data seed, and feature extraction uses evaluation
mode. The resulting independence is conditional on the fixed mathematical
universe, tasks, historical exclusion set, and sampling procedure; it does
not test a new task distribution.

Model configuration, data scale, optimizer, hyperparameters, supervision,
and the fixed 20,000-update endpoint are unchanged. The 256-dimensional,
four-layer configurations retain their original unequal parameter counts.
The runner saves configuration and source identities, actual initial
weights, starting random states, optimizer and random states at checkpoints,
and separate records of failures. Only completed artifacts with matching
identities can be skipped or resumed. Technical interruptions may resume
the same configuration; weak learning does not remove a prescribed
endpoint or trigger replacement seeds.

An engineering check used sixteen 40-update development copies on four
tasks, two encodings, and both architectures. Its extraction/analysis
adapter reused the first 128 validation and test anchors at $n=10$ from
the first new unit. These copies are excluded from the 256 formal
endpoints. This overlap was used to check execution and exact recovery,
not to select seeds, budgets, metrics, or a favorable geometric direction;
the registered scientific specification was unchanged. We therefore
retain the engineering record rather than describe every held-out anchor
as previously uninspected. An external interruption of the formal worker
was recovered from its saved 18,000-update optimizer/RNG state without
replacing the seed or changing the endpoint.

\paragraph{Analysis and scope.}
The main measurements retain the final normalized state at the end of the
input, before the task token, the original controls and metrics, and fixed
equal weights across all 21 lengths. Comparison A retains the separate
initial and trained correct and wrong scores and their decomposition.
Comparison B retains all fifteen candidate tasks, the mean of the fourteen
non-designated candidates, and the original strict ranking rule. A unique
first rank requires no higher score and no exact tie; undefined length
components are not replaced or reweighted. Under the within-architecture
shared initial state, raw Comparison B candidate scores coincide, up to
floating-point aggregation, before fitting. Candidate-specific answer
residualization does not share that zero reference.

The unit is the repetition level for summaries. The analysis reports every
unit's counts and continuous condition-level effects, followed by unit-level
means, sample standard deviations, ranges, and the directions and magnitudes
observed for each condition. It does not treat the 32 dependent conditions,
candidate pairs, or pooled counts as independent binomial trials. The
original unit remains identifiable as exploratory, and the added repetitions
follow a protocol fixed after viewing the original results. Independent
data and shared-weight starts between units do not test specialists with
independent initializations \emph{within} a unit; robustness to that changed
design remains a separate question.
