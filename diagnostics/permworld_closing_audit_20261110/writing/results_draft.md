# Results paragraphs for a revised TMLR draft

These paragraphs integrate the completed analyses; they are exploratory post-result diagnostics, not a retrospectively preregistered confirmation. Numerical support is in `claim_evidence_scope.csv` and the linked unit tables. The old manuscript has not been edited.

**Geometric selectivity depends on its control and reference.** In the existing four-unit analysis, the designated-transform advantage under within-length dual-answer residual U-CKA averaged 0.125783 across the selected 32 conditions, with 22 conditions positive in every new unit. This stricter advantage subtracts the strongest eligible alternative transformation and differs from the original one-wrong-transformation Bt. A training increment relative to shared initialization need not reflect increasing correct-pair similarity: the original analysis retained both score components and found that much of the increment reflected declining identity-pair similarity. The current audit exactly reproduced the E/D table. For GRU, the mean cycle-minus-one-line advantage was −0.031561 for pooled ordinary CKA and −0.108743 for within-length U-CKA, but +0.077271 after the specified additive dual-answer-mean control, positive in all four new units. Transformer showed a negative residual contrast on average (−0.059307), with three negative units and one positive unit. Residualization removes the stated additive means, not all answer-related information; these comparisons do not identify a causal encoding mechanism.

**Aligned position readout has training-task specificity in the tested pair.** Keeping the exact trained peaks probe and scaler fixed, complement-aligned trained valleys reached a full-test mean per-example position AUROC of 0.633139 ± 0.017600 across the four new one-line GRU units. The corresponding saved step-0 target and trained fixed_points target scored 0.502419 ± 0.002165 and 0.502716 ± 0.002018, respectively. Each target used its own fit-only means and the same-budget orthogonal mapping into trained peaks coordinates. The paired valleys-minus-step-0 difference was 0.130721 ± 0.019630, range [0.101474, 0.142217], and valleys-minus-fixed_points was 0.130424 ± 0.017055, range [0.105684, 0.143470]; both were positive in all four units. The mean margin over the best of the eleven common mapping controls was 0.113813 ± 0.019549. The original count-matched valleys mean 0.637309 remains a sensitivity result; full-test mean 0.633139 is primary here. This supports additional transferable position readout associated with the trained target task under the fitted correspondence. It does not establish that the networks naturally share a decoder or counting computation.

**Geometric and readout contrasts need not rank encodings alike.** The residual E/D CKA advantage was 0.019517 for one-line GRU and 0.096787 for cycle GRU. In contrast, the final-state position-readout margin over the best common map control was 0.010082 ± 0.001878 for one-line and 0.000150 ± 0.000971 for cycle, with the latter positive in only two of four units. Cycle's higher raw aligned AUROC did not produce the larger mapping-specific margin. CKA and AUROC have different definitions, fits and nuisance controls, so these numerical effects are not subtracted. The original predicted cross-task encoding double dissociation failed and remains a failed prediction.

**Frozen readouts can be composed without yielding a cross-specialist advantage.** In the previously completed composition test, the aligned two-specialist route scored lower than the same-model-twice baseline in every new unit on all three questions, under both full and matched supports. Full-test mean exact accuracy was 9.43% versus 14.84% for the longest monotone interval, 49.39% versus 52.29% for the longest interval containing a fixed point, and 53.50% versus 54.15% for turns before the first fixed point. Alignment improved the interval questions over unaligned transfer, and count-preserving position shuffles reduced performance where the supplied algorithms used those positions. These are results for analyst-supplied composition rules applied to supervised readouts, not evidence that the networks themselves invoke a reusable lemma.

**The selected local edit does not change the original count.** Across 80,640 existing formal directed cases, the registered one-dimensional L1 edit flipped the independent local checker at weighted rates of 98.8159–100% across unit cells, but changed the selected count in none of the cases under either late injection or current-token upper replay. This includes cumulative-count-matched donors. Whole-L1 replacement at the same interface produced requested relative ±1 changes at 99.743–100% across the eight early-condition cell means, so the interface can convey changes that this specific axis/dose does not reproduce. Complete-layer swaps mix state histories and do not locate a uniquely necessary counter layer.

**Projection and derivative measurements bound the interpretation.** The edit is a raw-recurrent-state Euclidean donor projection after converting the saved scaler/ridge coefficients back into raw coordinates; the ideal projection and actual rounded FP32 displacement are retained separately. At the baseline recipient state we differentiated m=z[c+s]−z[c] through the upper current-token replay and the original suffix, retaining the true recipient c+s goal for count-matched donors. Across the eight registered task/construction/sign cell means, projection energy was 0.6305–0.7195% of the donor-difference energy, while the gradient norm was 0.2396–0.3950. Thus the modal-count null is not a zero-gradient result. Mean actual margin changes ranged from -0.001746 to 0.002107 logit units, against baseline goal margins of -9.9544 to -9.1883; responses were not uniformly goal-positive. The condition-level tables retain both signs and donor constructions, every unit and all eight random directions. Fixed finite differences on a FP64 copy of the same smooth graph check derivative wiring, while FP32 no-edit and edited logits remain within the original tolerance of the saved paths. Actual margin changes use cached FP32 logits; individual sufficiently small changes can approach backend roundoff, so a discrepancy from g·δ does not by itself identify curvature or a mechanistic residual. Small projection energy, a small directional derivative or an angle statistic alone establishes neither absence of local information use nor a necessarily distributed causal code.

**Evidence levels remain distinct.** Geometric preference, recoverability after an explicitly fitted map, performance of an explicit composition pipeline and the response to a specific model-internal intervention answer different questions. Their numerical values are not a single effect shrinking across stages. In particular, the within-model intervention does not directly test computation shared across specialists. The repeated units jointly resample data, architecture-level initialization and training randomness; they do not separately identify those sources or cover unrelated specialist initializations, other task families or arbitrary model configurations.
