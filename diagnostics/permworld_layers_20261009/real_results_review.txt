Layer dependence
Across the 10 neural branches for the two inverse relations, all three primary metrics have positive Delta in the length means of all 3 sources.
However, the first block's final-token branch is not positive at every length: both relations have negative-Delta lengths.
For every final-token branch from the second block onward and every contextual-mean branch,
Delta is positive across all three sources, all 21 lengths, and all three primary metrics.
This supports that the signal is not limited to the final-layer readout, but not that it is equally strong at every position/length.
Bt for the block01 final token and each layer's pooling also varies by source/length; Delta cannot replace Bt.
Exceedance intermediate-layer final-token and pooling branches have larger dc; final-layer primary-position dc is smaller.
Large Delta for the descents relation mainly accompanies declining dw across layers. The two relations cannot share one absolute-convergence narrative.
The largest-effect layer was not made the primary analysis; the final layer remains the original primary position.

Input-encoding baselines
For the position-preserving genuine numeric-embedding baseline, both inverse relations have negative
source-mean Delta on all three primary metrics. CKA Delta is -0.120777 and -0.082482, respectively.
Thus flattening trained per-value embedding geometry alone does not reproduce the direction of positive hidden-layer increments.
This rules out the simple description “the same-direction effect is already entirely in per-value embeddings,” but
does not isolate the contributions of positional/sequential encoding, nonlinear contextual computation, or output information to hidden geometry.
It does not prove that hidden layers discover an independent algebraic mechanism.

Static one-hot features have no initialization/B0/Delta. Positional one-hot Gram matrices are theoretically invariant
under complementation, reversal, and inversion, giving pairing differences of 0; this follows from the isometric input construction.
The final-token embedding and unordered embedding mean are structural constants, with undefined metrics.
Floating-point rounding or numerous exact neighborhood ties for these constants are not interpreted as relation signals.

Answer and output associations remain possible
After answer stratification, output_prob CKA Delta remains +0.336123/+0.331151; KNN is also positive for both.
Thus changes in correct/wrong pairing preferences are not limited to hidden space; output geometry has same-direction signals.
Mean output-Procrustes Delta is positive for descents, but individual lengths can be negative;
mean output-Procrustes Delta is -0.200369 for exceedances, with the three sources spanning
[-0.868721,+0.974798], so robust agreement across all three output-control metrics cannot be claimed.
Procrustes can yield extremely large NRMSE when target projected energy is small; these results are fully retained,
without changing metrics, rank, or lengths based on results or directly subtracting their values from CKA.

Ordinary per-length controls and answer stratification differ in magnitude. For example, final-layer CKA Delta is:
descents within_length 0.993578 versus answer_strata 0.588965;
exceedances within_length 1.379408 versus answer_strata 0.641461.
Answer-associated structure accounts for substantial magnitude changes, but stratification does not remove all confidence, prediction errors,
or nonlinear answer information. Hidden Delta minus output Delta cannot estimate an independent mechanism's contribution.

Currently supported claim
For these existing checkpoints and fixed evaluation inputs, training changed relative transform-pairing
preferences compared with reconstructed initialization; direction, magnitude, and absolute correct-pair changes depend on relation, layer, and pooling.
Positive increments for two inverse relations have broad coverage across three metrics and most prespecified neural branches.
Simple positional embedding baselines do not reproduce same-direction increments, but output probabilities do,
so initialization-encoding geometry and output-related explanations remain incompletely distinguished.
The next encoding control with genuine shared step-0 should separate B0/Bt/dc/dw/Delta and retain task
accuracy and output controls; these results do not guarantee that encoding changes final relation preferences.
