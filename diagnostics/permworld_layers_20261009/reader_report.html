3. Input embeddings, answers, and output associations

Within a length, embeddings at the input-end token are identical; the unordered mean of numeric-token embeddings is also identical because every permutation contains exactly 1…n. Both geometries are mathematically degenerate and are explicitly recorded as undefined, without treating floating-point addition-order noise as input signal. This does not imply that downstream contextual means are degenerate.

Flattened position-preserving numeric-token embedding/one-hot baselines were fixed before analysis. They preserve input position–value pairings; within-length positional and punctuation additive terms are sample constants that cancel under centering. Learned embedding baselines can change during training; static one-hot features have no interpretable training mechanism. These input baselines diagnose whether a signal already exists in input representations, rather than proving that the network discovers additional algebraic relations.
